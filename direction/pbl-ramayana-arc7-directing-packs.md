# M1 — The Promise
**32.1 s · 16 shots · Dutt XVIII · the first film of a session · hard join into M2**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** a sage arrives, the king honours him, and — before hearing a word of what he wants — gives his word.

**Feel:** warmth. Generosity with no floor under it. This film must play **completely open**. Nothing here is ominous; the horror arrives retroactively, in M2, and only if M1 refuses to hint at it.

**The pull at the end:** the sage has not said it yet. What will he ask for?

## B · Performance

**Daśaratha** — *a host, glad to have a guest.*
- Warm, open-handed, unguarded. At 11 he gives his word the way people do when they mean it and haven't counted the cost.
- *Do not play:* hesitation, a flicker of doubt, a glance at the court. He has no idea what is coming, and neither should the audience.

**Viśvāmitra** — *a guest being honoured.* Patient, composed, unreadable — but never sinister.
- **Declared choice.** The text has him **thrilled** at the promise — his hair stands on end (18.1; Dutt XIX, *"with his down standing on end"*). We play restraint: at 14, only the smallest intake of breath. That is our choice. *If reversed:* at 14 let the pleasure show — the eyes brighten, the breath catches with delight. The source supports it fully.
- *Do not play:* mystery, a knowing look, concealment. He simply hasn't spoken yet.

**The court** — a stir at 03 that is curiosity, not alarm.
**Vasiṣṭha** at 10 — **unreadable, not uneasy.** The treatment calls this *"the only flicker of unease"*, which is foreshadowing, and M1 forbids foreshadowing. He is watching. That is all.

# M2 — The Ask
**42.1 s · 18 shots · Bāla 18.1–20 · hard join from M1 · continuous into M3**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** the sage has a problem — a rite broken by two rākṣasas, and a vow that stops him cursing them. He makes the king recommit to his promise. Then he asks: not for the army. For Rāma. For ten nights.

**Feel:** a slow narrowing. The film opens on a room and ends on one word.

**The pull at the end:** will the father give him?

## B · Performance

**Viśvāmitra** — *a man with a practical problem, asking for the one thing that solves it.*
- At 03 — *"keep your word"* — said plainly, as a courtesy between kings. Not a trap springing. The trap is only visible later.
- He reports the rākṣasas the way you'd report an illness. At 05 he raises two fingers, once — our gesture, not the text's.
- *Do not play:* sinister, sly, satisfied. If he is sinister, the reveal is cheap.
- The **declared choice** from M1 continues: the text has him thrilled as he begins (18.1). We play him level.

**Daśaratha** — *reassured, then relieved, then struck.*
- 02: his hand closed on the armrest — the last time he sits on the throne in the arc.
- 07–08: receiving the news of the rite, grim but unworried — this is a problem armies solve.
- 14–15: **he thinks he can pay this.** Relief, readiness. Play it; it makes 18.
- 17: the name lands on the unchanged face.
- **18 — the shock.** The text: he *"shook with a mighty sorrow"* and *"shook on his seat"* (Dutt XIX), then for a moment was *"as if senseless"* (19.1). Direction: a small **involuntary lurch** on the name, then the face freezes. The 8-second hold renders that moment of senselessness. The stillness is grounded; its length is ours.
- *Do not play:* a faint, a gasp, a hand to the chest.

# M3 — A Father Says No
**51.4 s · 23 shots · Bāla 18.9–19.11 · continuous from M2 · continuous into M4 · the arc's hero film**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** the father refuses. He offers his army, then himself, then anything at all — anything but Rāma. The sage has said Vasiṣṭha's consent would settle it; the king does not ask.

**Feel:** the end of a struggle. Love, not weakness. By the last beat the audience should be on the king's side and afraid for him at once.

**The pull at the end:** who are these rākṣasas he fears?

**The film you'd send to a stranger.** Its hero frame is 17 — the joined hands.

## B · Performance

**Daśaratha** — *shock settling → fear → kingly resolve → the break → the refusal.*
- 01: the shock from M2, settling.
- 05: **he rises**, cloth falling, and steps down off the dais. Dutt XIX has it: *"having regained his sense, he rose up."* From here he never returns to the throne.
- 07: fear arriving — the mouth working before speech.
- 11–14: **kingly resolve.** He offers the army outward to the hall, arm opening; then himself, a hand finding where a bow would hang. These are strong beats. He is still a king here.
- 16: resolve breaking.
- 17: **the palms come together** — a king doing this to nobody. Our staging, and any Indian viewer reads it instantly. The face is love, not weakness.
- 19: undone — close, breathing, nothing said.
- 21: **"He said no."** Three level words. This is the audition line for every narrator.
- 22: the hands fall to his sides. The refusal costs him the plea.
- *Do not play:* tears, collapse, a raised voice, a gesture toward the sage.

**Viśvāmitra** — *withholding, not absent.*
- He does not move for the whole film **except at 18**, where his eyes go down to the joined hands and come back up. His eyes only. That single movement is what makes the stillness read as a choice.

# M4 — The Name Behind Them
**48.7 s · 18 shots · Bāla 19.12–24 · joins continuous from M3, continuous into M5**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** the two rākṣasas are not the danger. Someone sends them — someone this king cannot face with his whole army. The problem has just become enormous.

**Feel:** a room going cold. The king came into this film hoping the threat was small enough to buy off. By the end he knows it isn't, and he refuses anyway — not from strength, but because he has nothing left to offer.

**The pull at the end:** the sage has now been refused twice. What does a man like this do when he's refused?

## B · Performance

**Daśaratha** — *hope → recognition → a contained collapse → refusal from exhaustion.*
- At 02 his chin lifts as he asks. He is hoping the answer is a name he can deal with. Play the hope; it makes the fall.
- He hears "Rāvaṇa" in the high wide (08). We are too far away to read his face, and that is the point. Do not cut in for a reaction.
- 12–16 is the whole performance. At 14 his **eyes drop to the floor** — the only large movement he makes in the film. At 16 they **come back up**. Between them, at 15, the joined hands press whiter. That's the admission, played in three frames.
- The second refusal (17) is **smaller than the first**. Quieter, flatter, tired. If it's bigger, it's wrong.
- *Do not play:* terror, trembling, tears, a hand to the head, a collapse. He is a king. The fear goes inward.

**Viśvāmitra** — *unchanged.*
- He delivers the name the way a physician delivers a diagnosis: slowly, completely, without enjoying it.
- *Do not play:* menace, satisfaction, a knowing look. He is not revealing a secret for effect. He is answering a question.

**The court** — one courtier's head lowers at 11. One. That is the room's entire reaction to the name.

# M5 — The Sage's Anger
**40.4 s · 16 shots · Bāla 20.1–4 · continuous from M4, continuous into M6**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** the sage names what just happened — a king gave his word and is taking it back — and he leaves.

**Feel:** the floor dropping out. Four films of a man who did not move, and now he does.

**The pull at the end:** the sage is walking out. Can anyone stop this?

## B · Performance

**Viśvāmitra** — *four films of stillness, broken once.*

**The creative choice, stated plainly.** The text has him **seized by wrath** — the earth shakes, fear enters the gods (20.1, 20.4). This film renders that wrath as **control**: the stillness breaking, a staff on stone, words weighed one at a time. That is our interpretation, not the text's. It is legitimate and it is declared. Share copy, captions and Ask must never call him *calm*.

- The anger arrives through the body before the words. At 03 he **rises** — the first movement on him in four films. Let the breath deepen visibly before he moves.
- The staff comes up (05) and down (06). He does not raise it high. It is not a weapon.
- 08–13: he speaks standing, level. Each word is placed, not thrown. The anger is in how little he wastes.
- *Do not play:* a raised voice, a pointed finger, narrowed eyes, a snarl, a step toward the king. He turns his head, then his back.

> **If the director reverses this choice** — plays the wrath hot, as the text has it — the change is: breath heavier from 03, the staff strike harder and with a tremor in the frame, the lines faster. The source supports that version fully. Nothing else in the film needs to change.

**Daśaratha** — at 07 his **eyes do not drop**. In M4 they did. Here he takes it standing. That is the only change, and it matters: this is the moment he stops being a man pleading. At 16, after the footsteps, **one exhale**.

**Vasiṣṭha** — at 11 he **shifts**: fabric moves, a held breath goes out. It is the first sign in the arc that he is going to act. Do not let him speak or rise here — that is M6.

# M6 — Vasiṣṭha's Word
**44.6 s · 22 shots · Bāla 20.5–21.3 · continuous from M5 · continuous into M7**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** Vasiṣṭha reminds the king that a man of his house keeps his word — and tells him the sage he has been refusing is Rāma's protection. The king calls his son. The rites are performed. He smells the crown of his son's head and gives him.

**This is the payoff of M3.** The sage said: *if Vasiṣṭha and the counsellors agree, let Rāma go.* Vasiṣṭha has now agreed.

**Feel:** **release, not defeat.** Both sources have the king glad at the end — Dutt XXII, *"with a glad heart."* The grief was films 3 to 5. This is the weight lifting.

**The pull at the end:** how does a father send his son?

## B · Performance

**Vasiṣṭha** — *he stands, and states.*
- He has been in the room for five films without moving. At 02 he steps forward. He never raises his voice, never gestures, never lectures. He is fulfilling the sage's condition, and he knows it.

**Daśaratha** — *spent → startled → addressed → recognising → released → glad.*
- 01: the king as M5 left him — **spent, alone after the sage's footsteps**. (The frozen expression note on this shot still describes the sage's back from an earlier version. The shot is the king.)
- 03: startled — someone who never moves has moved.
- 09: **recognition.** For the first time he sees the sage as protection, not as a petitioner.
- 12: his hands unclench — **at his sides**. He is not on the throne.
- 14: the first easy breath in four films.
- 15: he calls his son — resolve, not concession.
- 19: he bends and **smells the crown of his son's head**. A specific, old, deeply Indian act of a parent's love. Not a hand on a head.
- 21: **glad.** This is the correction the arc depends on. *Do not play him broken.*

**Viśvāmitra** — back still turned at 10. At 17 he turns back, slowly. **He does not smile.** No triumph.

**Rāma** — in 18 and 19 only as a bowed head. **His face is not shown.** His FIRST FACE REVEAL is M7/04.

**Kausalyā** — in 18, her hands only.

# M7 — Two Sons Leave
**40.4 s · 21 shots · Bāla 21.6 · continuous from M6 · the first exterior in the product**

## A · Story intent

**Understand:** they leave, in the order the text gives — the sage first, then Rāma, then Lakṣmaṇa.

**Feel:** air. Six films in one room, and now the room is behind us. It should feel like a loss nobody says out loud.

**The pull at the end:** the road runs out of frame. What does the sage give the boy on the road?

## B · Performance

**Rāma** — *his FIRST FACE REVEAL in the entire product, and it must be unremarkable on purpose.* M6 showed him only as a bowed head; this is the first time the audience sees his face.
- 04 is a boy, plainly framed. No low angle, no rim light, no wind, no push. Everything the audience has heard about him for six films sits on him without a single shot insisting on it.
- Side-locks visible, a bow carried unstrung (21.6).
- At 17 he **looks back once**, then faces forward. That is the only emotion he shows in the film — and it is our invention, not the text's.
- *Do not play:* resolve, destiny, a heroic set of the jaw, a smile at the camera.

**Lakṣmaṇa** — half a step behind, already. He watches his brother more than he watches the road.

**Daśaratha** — *he does not follow.* At 03 his hand rests on his son's shoulder at the threshold and lifts away. At 08 he watches. At 19, when we cut back to the hall, **he is still standing in the doorway**. He does not call out, does not raise a hand, does not weep.

**Kausalyā** — 06 is her hand letting go of the edge of her son's upper cloth. Her face is never shown. She is not this film's subject, and withholding her is what makes the moment land.

**Viśvāmitra** — already turned toward the road at 07. He has what he came for; he doesn't look back.
