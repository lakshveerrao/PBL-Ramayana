# PBL Ramayana — Arc 7 · Character Design Briefs
## Daśaratha · Viśvāmitra · Vasiṣṭha · Rāma · Lakṣmaṇa · Kausalyā's hands

**What this is.** A brief for each character the Arc 7 films need a model sheet for — the thing the studio generates or commissions against. Once a sheet is approved by a named person, **the approved sheet governs every frame**, and this brief has done its job.

**Authority.** These are CREATIVE PROPOSALS from the creative side. They sit on top of the frozen source package (v1.0.6), which fixes only what is true. Each brief separates three things and never blurs them:
- **The text says** — established by the source. Binding.
- **The world requires** — the material-world constraints every frame obeys. Binding.
- **We propose** — a design choice, with a recommendation and the reason. Yours to change.

---

## Part 1 — What every sheet must be

**Deliverables per character**
1. **Front** — full figure and head-and-shoulders
2. **Three-quarter** — the view most shots use
3. **Profile**
4. **In-world frame** — the character in the hall's light: lattice from frame-left, one brass lamp
5. **Expression row** — the states the arc needs, listed in each brief
6. **Detail plates** — hands for everyone; ornament and headdress close-ups where they exist

**The four views establish the angle axis only.** Lighting, distance and expression are proven later, in the first renders of M1 — which is why M1 is the visual checkpoint.

**Rules every sheet obeys**
- **Cloth is woven, cut from the loom, and draped.** Never tailored, never buttoned, never a stitched blouse or shirt. The antarīya at the waist, the uttarīya over the shoulder. The single fastest test of any sheet: *does the cloth hang like cloth, or sit like a garment?*
- **Ornament from the relief vocabulary** — Bharhut, Sanchi, Amarāvatī: heavy kuṇḍala earrings that pull the lobe, flat collars and torques, armlets above the bicep, bangles. Gold that gleams, never glitters. No faceted gems. No European regalia.
- **Faces from the real range of the subcontinent.** Not one beauty standard. Nose bridges, eye shapes, jaws and cheeks that vary. Pores, oil sheen, sun on skin that has lived outdoors, age in the neck and hands.
- **Never sharpen features to read "noble" or broaden them to read "lowly."** That is caste and race coding, and it is forbidden.
- **Skin is never lightened** — not on the sheet, not in any later grade. The approved sheet sets each character's skin; nothing afterwards may lift it.
- **Bodies are real.** No gym physiques. Everyone barefoot on stone.

**The eye test for every candidate sheet:** is the cloth draped? Is there a dome, an arch or marble anywhere behind him? Is the skin at least as deep as intended? Does the ornament look like a temple relief or a jewellery shop?

**Acceptance.** A sheet is approved when:
- a stranger, shown the four views shuffled, says it is one person;
- the age reads correctly at thumbnail size;
- the character can't be mistaken for another principal at a glance — especially **Viśvāmitra against Vasiṣṭha**, and **Rāma against Lakṣmaṇa from behind**;
- it passes the eye test above;
- a named person signs it off.

## Part 2 — The briefs

### DAŚARATHA · King of Ayodhyā
**In frame:** M1 through M7 — the most shots of anyone. **Plates that carry the arc:** his shock (M2/18 → M3/01), the silent king (M3/20 → M3/21, M4/17), the spent king (M5/16 → M6/01, 05).

**The text says**
- He is **old** — Rāma is the son of his old age.
- He is a king of the line of Ikṣvāku, with an army he offers to lead himself.
- On hearing the request he **shakes on his seat** in fear and sorrow; for a moment he is as if senseless (18.20, 19.1).
- At the giving he is **glad** — *"with a glad heart"* (21.3, Dutt XXII).
- *"Sixty thousand years"* is an epic idiom for great age, not a number to render.

**The world requires**
- Draped royal dress: antarīya and uttarīya, fine, with a woven border.
- Ornament from the reliefs: kuṇḍala, a flat collar, armlets, bangles, one ring.

**We propose**
- **Age:** late sixties in appearance. A broad frame gone soft — a man long a king, who has not fought in years. Age in the hands and neck, not only the face.
- **Hair:** long, oiled, silver-white, bound. A full beard, grey-white, trimmed.
- **Complexion:** warm mid-brown.
- **Dress:** a near-white antarīya; a deep red uttarīya with a thin woven gold border. The red becomes the throne's cloth too — the king and his seat share a colour until he leaves it.
- **Headdress — your decision.**
  - **Option A (recommended): the relief-period royal headdress** — a wound turban with a crest ornament, as kings wear on the Bharhut and Sanchi reliefs. It's the most period-faithful choice available, and almost no screen Rāmāyaṇa has used it.
  - **Option B: a low mukuṭa** — a band rising to a modest crest, recognisable to audiences from later iconography.
  - **Never:** a peaked European crown, a tiara, a jewelled tower.

**The face must carry**
A face that has smiled a great deal and isn't smiling now. Heavy-lidded, deep-set eyes. The arc asks more range of him than of anyone:

| State | Where |
|---|---|
| Warm, a glad host; generous, unthinking; at ease | M1 |
| Grim but unworried; relief — he thinks he can pay | M2/07–15 |
| **Frozen shock** — a moment as if senseless | M2/18 |
| Fear arriving; kingly resolve; resolve breaking | M3 |
| **Undone** — close, breathing | M3/19 |
| Hope; **eyes dropped to the floor**; eyes back up | M4 |
| Taking it standing; spent, one exhale | M5 |
| Startled; **recognition**; the first easy breath | M6 |
| **Glad** — not broken | M6/21 |
| Watching, staying | M7 |

**Physical states:** seated on the throne (M1–M2 only) · standing (from M3/05) · bending to his son's crown (M6/19) · palms joined (M3/17).

**Never:** tears streaming · a hand clutched to the chest · a faint to the floor · a youthful face · a sword at his hip · a robe.

### VIŚVĀMITRA · The sage
**In frame:** M1 through M7. **The most important plate in the arc is his:** the still sage, seated (M2/03), is reused or cropped in **fourteen shots across four films**. Design him for stillness first.

**The text says**
- An ascetic, under an observance that stops him cursing those who break his rite.
- Of the line of Kuśika. *(That he was once a king is told in a later arc — in Arc 7 it may show in his bearing, never be stated.)*
- **Thrilled** when the king gives his word — his hair stands on end (18.1).
- **Seized by wrath** at the refusal (20.1, 20.4).

**The world requires**
- Ascetic dress: uncut, undyed or ochre, draped. Often one cloth.
- A plain wooden staff — no carving, no finial.

**We propose**
- **Age:** sixties. Lean and physically hard — the shoulders of a man who once trained.
- **Hair:** matted, piled high (jaṭā). Ash-grey beard.
- **Complexion:** deep warm brown, sun-weathered. Ash on the skin.
- **Dress:** ochre cloth at the waist; a single upper cloth; bare torso otherwise. A string of rudrākṣa — worn, never displayed.
- **The staff:** dark, dense wood, shoulder-height, worn pale at the grip, blunt and splayed at the base from years on stone. It is planted at his right through M1–M4.
- **The one thing to get right:** his eyes **rest** on what they look at. They do not search.

**The face must carry**

| State | Where |
|---|---|
| **Seated, composed, unreadable — the still sage** | M1–M4, and 14 reuses |
| About to speak — a smallest intake of breath | M1/14 |
| Two fingers raised, once | M2/05 |
| **Eyes down to the king's hands, and back up** | M3/18 |
| Rising | M5/03 |
| Standing, speaking — anger as control | M5/08 |
| Walking away, back to camera | M5/14 |
| Turning back, slowly; no smile | M6/17 |
| At the door, facing the road; walking out first | M7 |

**Must be distinct from Vasiṣṭha at a glance.** Viśvāmitra: matted, ash-grey, lean, sun-dark, ochre. Vasiṣṭha: combed, white, softer, undyed white.

**Never:** a glow or aura · floating · wind in the hair · a trident · a rosary held up · a beatific smile · **villain coding** — narrowed eyes, a shadowed face, a low menacing angle. He is a man with a problem, not a threat.

### VASIṢṬHA · Family priest of the Raghus
**In frame:** M1, M2, M5, M6. **Plate that carries the arc:** Vasiṣṭha standing (M6/02 → 04, 06, 08, 11).

**The text says**
- The family priest of the house.
- He counsels the king to keep his word: one who promises and does not act loses his merit (20.6–8).
- He tells the king the sage is Rāma's protection (20.9).
- He utters the mantras at the blessing rite before the giving (21.2).

**The world requires**
- Brahmin dress: undyed white, draped, one shoulder bare. The sacred thread across the chest.
- Almost no ornament.

**We propose**
- **Age:** older than the king — seventies.
- **Hair:** white, combed, tied at the crown. A white beard cut short. Clean where Viśvāmitra is matted.
- **Complexion:** warm mid-brown, aged, softer than the sage's.
- **Ornament:** one plain ring and the thread. Nothing else.
- **Bearing:** he sits lower than everyone and is listened to by all of them. When he finally stands, the whole room registers it.

**The face must carry**

| State | Where |
|---|---|
| Seated among the ministers — **watching, unreadable, not uneasy** | M1/10, M2/11 |
| Shifting; a held breath released | M5/11 |
| Rising | M5/14 |
| **Standing, stating** — plain, unraised | M6 |
| The long look, king to priest | M6/13 |
| A raised hand over a bowed head — the rite | M6/18 |
| Eyes closed — it is done | M6/20 |

**Never:** a staff · a crown · a throne of his own · an oracular expression · a raised voice · standing over the king.

### RĀMA · The boy
**In frame:** M6 (a bowed head only, **face withheld**) and M7. **M7/04 is his FIRST FACE REVEAL** — the first time anyone sees his face in the product.

**The text says**
- **Not yet sixteen** — *as his father says it* (19.2). It is Daśaratha's statement, and the design should agree with it.
- He wears the **boy's side-locks** — *kākapakṣadhara* — both when he is asked for (18.8) and as he leaves (21.6). The clearest sign in the arc that a child is being given.
- He carries a **bow** as he leaves — *dhanvī* (21.6).
- **His colour is not established by the text we hold.** No complexion may be presented as mandated by the source.

**The world requires**
- Simple uncut cloth. Minimal ornament — a prince leaving on foot, not at court.
- The bow: recurved, wood and horn, **unstrung**, carried.

**We propose**
- **Age:** fifteen. Slight, not yet filled out. A boy — not a youth, and never a man's body in a boy's role.
- **Hair:** long, with the **side-locks clearly visible** from every angle, including from behind. They are what tells the audience how young he is.
- **Complexion — your decision, and it must be deliberate.**
  - **Recommended: a deep brown.** The long tradition holds Rāma as *śyāma* — dark (Tulsīdās; and the older iconographic habit, whose blue we exclude as a later convention). A deep brown honours that tradition within the real human range.
  - Whatever is chosen is **ours, from tradition — never presented as what the text says.** No caption, narration or share copy may state his colour.
- **Dress:** plain, undyed, shorter at the knee for walking. A small quiver at the back. Bare feet.
- **Face:** open, unguarded, a boy's. Neither sweet nor stern.

**The face must carry**

| State | Where |
|---|---|
| Head bowed, face **not shown** — the rite; the crown | M6/18, 19 |
| Feet, from behind | M7/02 |
| **FIRST FACE REVEAL — plain, a boy** | M7/04 |
| Crossing the threshold, from behind | M7/10 |
| Walking in order, from behind — side-locks and bow read | M7/14, 15 |
| **One look back** — the only emotion he shows | M7/17 |

**The from-behind view matters as much as the front.** In seven of his M7 shots we see his back. The side-locks and the bow must identify him from behind.

**Never:** a heroic image — low angle, rim light, wind · a halo · blue skin · a crown · a drawn bow · a smile to camera · a man's physique.

### LAKṢMAṆA · The brother
**In frame:** M7 only.

**The text says**
- Sumitrā's son.
- He **follows** Rāma out (21.6). The order is fixed: the sage, then Rāma, then Lakṣmaṇa.

**The world requires**
- Plain uncut cloth, like his brother's. Bare feet.

**We propose**
- **Age:** a year or so younger than Rāma — it shows in the jaw, not the height.
- **Build:** slightly sturdier. Where Rāma is quick, he is planted.
- **Hair:** long; side-locks too, but worn differently enough to tell them apart from behind — tied lower, or fuller.
- **Complexion — your decision.** Tulsīdās pairs the brothers *śyāma* and *gaura* — dark and fair. If you follow that contrast, Lakṣmaṇa sits lighter than Rāma, **still within the real Indian range**, and with no suggestion that lighter means better. If you don't, make them distinguishable by build and hair instead.
- **Face:** rounder than Rāma's, a heavier brow, a mouth that sets rather than softens. The face of someone who has already decided.
- **Carries:** his own bow, unstrung; an archer's finger guard.

**The face must carry**

| State | Where |
|---|---|
| Feet, from behind | M7/02 |
| Beside Rāma, half a step behind — **watching his brother, not the road** | M7/05 |
| Crossing, from behind | M7/11 |
| Walking, last in the order | M7/14, 21 |

**Must be distinct from Rāma from behind** — build, hair, the way he carries his bow.

**Never:** ahead of Rāma in frame · a heroic beat of his own · a drawn bow · speaking.

---

### KAUSALYĀ · Hands only
**In frame:** M6/18 (the blessing rite, her hands beside the father's) and M7/06. **Her face is never shown.** She isn't these films' subject, and withholding her is what makes her moments land.

**The text says**
- The mother performs the auspicious rite before the giving (21.2).

**We propose — a hands sheet, not a character sheet**
- **Age:** a queen and the mother of a fifteen-year-old — late thirties to forties, a generation younger than the king. Her hands show it.
- **Complexion:** warm brown, consistent with a woman of the court.
- **Ornament:** bangles, several — gold, and one of conch shell. One or two rings. Nothing that glitters.
- **Cloth at the wrist:** the edge of her draped upper cloth, dyed and bordered. **No sleeve** — nothing she wears is stitched.
- **Two plates:** her hands raised in the rite (M6/18); her hand **letting go of the edge of her son's upper cloth** (M7/06).

**Never:** a face · a stitched sleeve · jewellery that reads as a shop window.

## Part 3 — Making the sheets

### Route A — generate them (fits "everything AI")
1. **Anchor first.** For each character, generate 8–12 front-view candidates from the brief. Choose one. **That front view is the anchor.**
2. **Build the sheet from the anchor.** Generate the three-quarter, profile and in-world views conditioned on the anchor — never on another generated view. Drift compounds: a variation of a variation is a cousin.
3. **Expression row from the sheet**, not from scratch.
4. **Run the acceptance test** in Part 1. Reject and regenerate anything that fails the eye test, however good it looks otherwise.
5. **Approve by name.** Only then does the consistency gate open for that character.

**Order:** Viśvāmitra first — the still sage carries fourteen shots, and M1's checkpoint depends on him. Then Daśaratha, then Vasiṣṭha, Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, Kausalyā's hands.

**Every candidate prompt carries** the negative prompt from `render_policy.json` and the world rules above. The one line that must be in every character prompt: **"uncut draped handloom — no stitched garment."**

### Route B — commission an artist
Supply this document and the relief references it names (Bharhut, Sanchi, Amarāvatī, Ajanta). Deliverables as in Part 1, at production resolution. **Work-for-hire, company-owned**, with the right to use the sheets as generation references.

### Either way
A sheet can be revised later — but every frame generated from it inherits the revision, so **decide the headdress and the two complexions before generating anything else.**

## Part 4 — Decisions to make before the first sheet

1. **Daśaratha's headdress** — the relief-period royal turban (recommended) or a low mukuṭa.
2. **Rāma's complexion** — deep brown, from the tradition of Rāma as dark (recommended). Declared as our choice, never as the text's.
3. **Lakṣmaṇa against Rāma** — follow Tulsīdās's dark-and-fair contrast, or distinguish them by build and hair alone.
4. **Route** — generate (A) or commission (B).
